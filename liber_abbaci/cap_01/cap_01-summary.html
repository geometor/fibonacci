CAPITULUM I SUMMARY — THE NINE HINDU NUMERALS & PLACE VALUE¶

Structural Synopsis of Boncompagni Vol. I, pp. 1–10 (fol. 1r–4r)

English Edition · Latin (1202/1228 / Boncompagni 1857) · GEOMETOR Notes

Overview¶

Capitulum I of Liber Abbaci introduces Western Europe to positional base-10 Indo-Arabic arithmetic. Fibonacci establishes the nine Hindu glyphs ($9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1$), the zero symbol ($0$, zephirum), right-to-left decimal hierarchy, and medieval finger calculation (computus digitorum), providing the foundation upon which all subsequent fifteen chapters rest.

The Zephirum ($0$) as Positional Cipher¶

The Arabic ṣifr (transliterated by Leonardo as zephirum, source of both English zero and cipher) signifies “empty” or “void”. Unlike Roman or Greek alphabetic notations, which require new symbols for higher powers (I, X, C, M; $\alpha, \iota, \rho$), the positional zero allows nine glyphs to represent arbitrarily large integers with zero ambiguity:

$$\text{Value}(d_k d_{k-1} \dots d_1 d_0) = \sum_{j=0}^k d_j \cdot 10^j, \quad d_j \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots, 9\}.$$

Finger Reckoning (Computus Digitorum)¶

Leonardo describes the traditional Mediterranean and Venerable Bede finger-reckoning system to hold intermediate sums during abacus computation:

  • Left Hand (Fingers): Units ($1..9$) bent upon the palm; Tens ($10..90$) formed by thumb and index postures.

  • Right Hand (Fingers): Hundreds ($100..900$) mirrored from the left-hand tens; Thousands ($1{,}000..9{,}000$) mirrored from the left-hand units.

Structure of the Fifteen Chapters of Liber Abbaci¶

  1. Capitulum I: Knowledge of the nine Indian figures and the zephirum; finger reckoning.

  2. Capitulum II: Multiplication of integers.

  3. Capitulum III: Addition of integers.

  4. Capitulum IV: Subtraction of smaller numbers from greater.

  5. Capitulum V: Division of integers.

  6. Capitulum VI: Multiplication of integers with fractions.

  7. Capitulum VII: Addition, subtraction, and division of integers with fractions.

  8. Capitulum VIII: Buying and selling of merchandise (Rule of Three).

  9. Capitulum IX: Barter of goods and monetary exchange.

  10. Capitulum X: Partnerships and companies (societates).

  11. Capitulum XI: Alligation of alloys and coinage (consolamen monetarum).

  12. Capitulum XII: Solutions to numerous mathematical problems and puzzles (including the Rabbit Problem, Part 7).

  13. Capitulum XIII: The Rule of Double False Position (regula elcataym).

  14. Capitulum XIV: Finding square and cube roots, binomials and apotomes (Euclid Book X).

  15. Capitulum XV: Geometrical proportions, algebra and almucabala (al-jabr wa’l-muqābala).
