NOTES — DISTINCTIO III: MENSURATION OF FIELDS, HERON’S PROOF & CIRCLE BOUNDS¶

Philological Commentary, Classical Lineage, and Exact Symbolic Verification

English Edition · Latin (1220 / Boncompagni 1862) · Summary

Section 1: The (13, 14, 15) Scalene Benchmark & Rational Trigonometry¶

Triangle $(13, 14, 15)$ is the canonical medieval Heronian triangle. Its perpendicular altitude splits base $14$ into rational segments $5$ and $9$, creating two adjacent integer right triangles $(5, 12, 13)$ and $(9, 12, 15)$. Leonardo uses this rational decomposition to demonstrate both the Euclidean casus method [II. 13] and the semiperimeter product rule.

Section 2: The Transmission of Heron’s Proof via the Banū Mūsā¶

While the formula $\Delta = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$ originated with Hero of Alexandria (Metrica I. 8), Hero’s Greek text was lost to medieval Europe until Schöne’s 1896 discovery in Constantinople. Leonardo received the proof through Gerard of Cremona’s 12th-century Latin translation of the Verba Filiorum of the Banū Mūsā (banu-musa:verba). Leonardo’s exposition is clearer than the Arabic-Latin translation, emphasizing the incenter $T$ and the semiperimeter line $BL$.

Symbolic verification in geometor.fibonacci:

from geometor.fibonacci import heron_area_exact, construct_heron_incenter_proof

        s, exc, sq, area = heron_area_exact(13, 14, 15)
        assert s == 21
        assert exc == (8, 7, 6)
        assert area == 84

        proof = construct_heron_incenter_proof()
        assert proof.incenter_t.x == 6
        assert proof.incenter_t.y == 4
        assert proof.inradius == 4

Section 3: Quadrilateral Fallacies & Agromensorial Caution¶

Leonardo issues a sharp warning against Roman agrimensores who computed quadrilateral areas by $\frac{a+c}{2} \cdot \frac{b+d}{2}$. He proves by [I. 28] and [I. 41] that this formula is strictly an upper bound, yielding exact results if and only if the quadrilateral is rectangular.

Section 4: The 96-gon Bounds & Fibonacci’s Ratio 864 : 275¶

In demonstrating Archimedes’ circle bounds (archimedes:circle), Leonardo avoids large 5-digit square roots by scaling diameter $d = 1440$ ($r = 720$). His harmonic mean:

$$\frac{1440}{458\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{864}{275} \approx 3.141818\dots$$

yields an approximation whose error is under $0.007\%$, closer to $\pi$ than Archimedes’ upper bound $\frac{22}{7} \approx 3.142857$.
