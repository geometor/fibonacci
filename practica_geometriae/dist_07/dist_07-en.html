DISTINCTIO VII — SURVEYING WITH THE QUADRANS¶

Optical Surveying of Heights, Depths, and Planar Distances with and without the Quadrans

Source Text: fibonacci:pg-7 (Boncompagni Vol. II, pp. 202–207 · fol. 130v–134r) · Latin (1220 / Boncompagni 1862) · Summary · GEOMETOR Notes

Surveying Heights & Distances with the Quadrans¶

Latin Locus: Distinctio VII — De Altitudinibus et Quadrante (pp. 202–207) · Commentary: Notes on Optical Similar Triangles

Here begins the Seventh Distinction, on finding the heights of elevated things, the depths of wells and valleys, and planar distances, with and without the instrument called the quadrant (quadrans, quem quidem oroscopum vocant).

  1. Without an Astrolabe or Quadrant: Set up a vertical rod of known height, or place a horizontal plane mirror (or vessel of water) upon the ground and step backward until the summit of the tower appears at the center of reflection; by equal angles of incidence and reflection (or parallel rays of the sun), the observer’s eye-height and tower height form equiangular right triangles, [VI. 4] giving:

    $$\frac{\text{Tower Height}}{\text{Tower Distance}} = \frac{\text{Eye Height}}{\text{Mirror Distance}}.$$

  2. With the *Quadrans* Shadow Square: Within the quarter-circle limb (DI) centered at sighting apex D, inscribe the square DHAI divided along both its horizontal umbra recta side IA and vertical umbra versa side AH into $12$ equal points (puncta).

    • When the weighted plumb line hangs along the diagonal DA (meeting the arc at B at $45^\circ$), the horizontal distance from the observer’s foot to the base of the tower equals the tower’s height above the observer’s eye.

    • When sighting a steeper tower, the plumb line DP (extended as DC to C) cuts umbra recta IA at P (for example, at $IP = 8$ puncta out of $DI = 12$). By similar triangles [VI. 4], the ratio of horizontal distance to vertical elevation is $8 : 12 = 2 : 3$; hence at distance $40$ perticae (and eye-height $1$ pertica), the tower height is $\frac{12}{8} \times 40 + 1 = 61$ perticae. Conversely, for shallow angles where the plumb line cuts umbra versa AH at $v$ puncta, elevation to distance is $v : 12$.

Thus all inaccessible heights, depths, and distances are determined by optical geometry.
