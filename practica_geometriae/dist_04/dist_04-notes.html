NOTES — DISTINCTIO IV: DIVISION OF PLANAR FIGURES AMONG CONSORTS¶

Philological Commentary, Classical Lineage, and Exact Symbolic Verification

English Edition · Latin (1220 / Boncompagni 1862) · Summary

Section 1: Euclid’s Lost On Divisions of Figures (De Divisionibus)¶

Distinctio IV is Leonardo’s most historically significant transmission. Euclid composed a specialized treatise On Divisions of Figures ($\Pi\epsilon\rho\grave{\iota}\ \delta\iota\alpha\iota\rho\acute{\epsilon}\sigma\epsilon\omega\nu\ \beta\iota\beta\lambda\acute{\iota}o\nu$), referenced by Proclus but lost in the original Greek. Leonardo’s text preserved the Euclidean methods for medieval Europe alongside Abraham bar Hiyya (Savasorda, Hibbur ha-Meshihah ve-ha-Tishboret, translated into Latin as Liber Embadorum in 1145 by Plato of Tivoli).

Section 2: Semicircle Mean Proportional & Euclid VI. 19¶

To divide a triangle parallel to its base in ratio $p : q$, Leonardo relies on [VI. 19]: similar plane figures have areas in the duplicate ratio of corresponding sides. The semicircle chord construction $AL = \sqrt{AM \cdot AB}$ solves the mean proportional $AB : AL = AL : AM$ mechanically using compass and straightedge, producing quadratic irrationals ($\sqrt{50}, \sqrt{72}, \sqrt{98}$) with zero numerical floating-point error.

Exact symbolic verification in geometor.fibonacci:

from geometor.fibonacci import construct_triangle_parallel_division
        import sympy as sp

        par = construct_triangle_parallel_division(p=1, q=1, preset="10-14-12")
        assert par.ae_quadrance == sp.Integer(50)
        assert par.az_quadrance == sp.Integer(72)
        assert par.ez_quadrance == sp.Integer(98)
        assert sp.simplify(par.area_aez - par.area_ebgz) == 0

Section 3: Centroid Partition & Archimedes’ Statics¶

The concurrency of the three medians at $X(2)$ and the resulting three equal area shares ($\Delta_1 = \Delta_2 = \Delta_3 = \frac{1}{3} \Delta$) connects Euclidean geometry directly to Archimedes’ On the Equilibrium of Planes I. 14. Leonardo proves concurrency purely through planar Euclidean transformations, without requiring the center of gravity apparatus.
