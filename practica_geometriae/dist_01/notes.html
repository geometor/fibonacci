NOTES — DISTINCTIO I: PROLOGUE, PISAN METROLOGY & RECTANGULAR FIELDS¶

Philological Commentary, Classical Lineage, and Exact Symbolic Verification

English Edition · Latin (1220 / Boncompagni 1862) · Summary

Section 1: The Dedication to Master Dominicus¶

The recipient of Practica Geometriae, Master Dominicus (Dominicus magister), was a prominent scholar and imperial notary in Pisa during the court visits of Emperor Frederick II. Leonardo characterizes his work as harmonizing two traditions:

  1. Demonstrative Geometrical Theory: Rooted in Euclid’s Elements (euclid:elements) and Archimedes’ circle quadrature.

  2. Lay Practical Measurement: The operational practice of surveyors (secundum vulgarem consuetudinem, quasi laicali more).

Section 2: The Pisan 6 × 6 Pertica System¶

Unlike modern metric or pure decimal systems, medieval Italian land mensuration utilized a mixed radix lattice. The Pisan system was governed by:

  • \(1 \text{ pertica} = 6 \text{ pedes}\)

  • \(1 \text{ pes} = 18 \text{ unciae}\) (points)

  • \(1 \text{ pertica} = 108 \text{ unciae}\)

  • \(1 \text{ pertica superficialis} = 36 \text{ denarii} = 36 \text{ sq. pedes}\)

  • \(1 \text{ panorum} = 5\frac{1}{2} \text{ perticae} = 198 \text{ denarii}\)

  • \(1 \text{ stariorum} = 12 \text{ panora} = 66 \text{ perticae} = 2{,}376 \text{ denarii}\)

Exact symbolic verification in geometor.fibonacci:

from geometor.fibonacci import PisanLinearMeasure, multiply_pisan_lengths, decompose_pisan_area
        import sympy as sp

        width = PisanLinearMeasure(perticae=7)
        depth = PisanLinearMeasure(perticae=9, pedes=2, unciae=sp.Rational(72, 7))
        prod = multiply_pisan_lengths(width, depth)
        assert prod.total_perticae == sp.Integer(66)
        assert prod.stariora == 1

Section 3: Geometric Algebra of Book II¶

Leonardo embeds Euclid’s Book II propositions directly into the surveyor’s art. Proposition [II. 4] gives the algebraic basis for rectangular expansions \((a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\), while [II. 5–6] provide the geometric foundation for solving quadratic equations by completing the square without symbolic notation.
