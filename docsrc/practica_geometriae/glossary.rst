Glossarium Technicum (Technical Glossary)
=========================================

.. rubric:: *Lexicon of Pisan Agrarian Metrology, Euclidean Geometry, and Graeco-Arabic Algebra*

Every inline ``:term:`` tag across ``geometor-fibonacci`` links directly to its ``#term-<slug>`` anchor below.

----

.. _term-pertica:

pertica (pertica pisana) — Pisan surveying rod
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (Pisan Metrology)
* **English Rendering**: *Pisan surveying rod (linear or superficial)*

Fundamental Pisan unit of land measurement: $1$ linear *pertica* $= 6$ linear *pedes* $= 108$ *unciae* (*puncta*); $1$ superficial (square) *pertica* $= 6$ superficial *pedes* $= 36$ *denarii* $= 108$ superficial *unciae* (*Practica Geometriae*, *Introductoria*, p. 3).

.. _term-pes:

pes (linealis / superficialis) — Pisan foot
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (Pisan Metrology)
* **English Rendering**: *Pisan foot ($1/6$ of a pertica)*

As a linear measure, $1/6$ of a *pertica* ($= 18$ *unciae*). As a superficial strip (*pes terrae*), a rectangle $1$ *pertica* ($6$ linear *pedes*) long by $1$ linear *pes* wide, equal to $1/6$ of a square *pertica* or $6$ *denarii* (*Introductoria*, p. 3).

.. _term-uncia:

uncia / punctum — inch or point
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (Pisan Metrology)
* **English Rendering**: *inch or point ($1/18$ of a Pisan pes; $1/108$ of a pertica)*

Fine subdivision of the Pisan rod: $18$ linear *unciae* make $1$ linear *pes*, and $108$ linear *unciae* make $1$ *pertica*. Superficially, $18$ superficial *unciae* make $1$ superficial *pes* (*Introductoria*, p. 3).

.. _term-denarius:

denarius de mensura — measuring denarius
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (Pisan Metrology)
* **English Rendering**: *measuring denarius ($1$ square pes $= 1/36$ square pertica)*

Unit square of side $1$ linear *pes* inside the $6 \times 6$ grid of the square *pertica*, so that $6$ *denarii* equal $1$ superficial *pes* and $36$ *denarii* equal $1$ superficial *pertica* (*Distinctio I*, pp. 3–5).

.. _term-panorum:

panorum — Pisan panorum ($5\frac{1}{2}$ square perticae)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (Pisan Metrology)
* **English Rendering**: *panorum ($5\frac{1}{2}$ square perticae)*

Pisan agrarian land measure equal to $5\frac{1}{2}$ superficial *perticae* ($= 1/12$ of a *stariorum*), used throughout the episcopate of Pisa (*Introductoria*, p. 3).

.. _term-stariorum:

stariorum — Pisan stariorum ($66$ square perticae)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (Pisan Metrology)
* **English Rendering**: *stariorum ($12$ panora $= 66$ square perticae)*

Principal Pisan grain-seeding field area equal to $12$ *panora* or $66$ superficial *perticae*; $24$ *stariora* constitute $1$ *modiorum* ($= 1584$ square *perticae*) (*Introductoria*, pp. 3–5).

.. _term-embadum:

embadum — planar area / surface content
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (from Greek *embadon* via Arabic/Hebrew)
* **English Rendering**: *planar area / surface content (or squared side)*

Standard Graeco-Latin technical term across Savasorda and Fibonacci for the superficial content (area) of a plane figure, and by extension the square built on the edge of a polyhedron (*embadum lateris*, *Distinctio III & VI*).

.. _term-cathetus:

cathetus — perpendicular altitude
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Latin (from Greek *kathetos*)
* **English Rendering**: *perpendicular altitude*

Perpendicular line dropped from a vertex of a triangle, pyramid, or cone onto the opposite base line or base plane (*linea perpendicularis, quae cathetus appellatur*, *Distinctio III*, p. 39).

.. _term-casus:

casus (maior / minor) — base projection segment
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin
* **English Rendering**: *projection segment cut on the base by the altitude*

Each of the two segments into which the foot of the *cathetus* divides the base of a triangle; on the $(13, 14, 15)$ triangle with base $14$, the minor *casus* is $5$, the major *casus* is $9$, and the *cathetus* is $12$ (*Distinctio III*, p. 39).

.. _term-consortes:

consortes — co-heirs / landholding partners
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Latin (Legal & Agrarian)
* **English Rendering**: *co-heirs / joint landholding partners*

Partners or co-owners sharing a field, orchard, well, or boundary access in fixed rational shares (e.g. halves, thirds, or $p : q$), motivating the exact Euclidean division constructions of *Distinctio IV* (*de divisione camporum inter consortes*, p. 110).

.. _term-media-et-extrema-proportio:

media et extrema proportio — extreme and mean ratio ($\phi$)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Latin (Euclid VI.def.3 / II.11 / XIII.1–18)
* **English Rendering**: *extreme and mean ratio (the golden section $\phi$)*

Division of a segment $BE$ at $G$ so that the whole $BE$ is to the greater part $BG$ as $BG$ is to the lesser part $GE$ ($BE : BG = BG : GE = \phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$). Used by Fibonacci in *Distinctio VI* (pp. 196–202) to construct the dodecahedron and icosahedron and in *Distinctio VIII* (pp. 207–208) for the pentagon and decagon.

.. _term-res:

res / radix — algebraic unknown ($x$)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (from Arabic *shayʾ* / *jidhr*)
* **English Rendering**: *the unknown quantity $x$ ('thing' or 'root')*

First-degree algebraic unknown $x$ in al-Khwārizmī's and Fibonacci's rhetorical algebra (*Distinctio VIII*, pp. 207–224; *Liber Abaci* Cap. XV).

.. _term-census:

census — square of the unknown ($x^2$)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (from Arabic *māl*)
* **English Rendering**: *the square of the unknown ($x^2$)*

Second-degree algebraic power $x^2$ ('wealth/square'); doubled in *Distinctio VIII* as *census census* for the fourth power $x^4$ in biquadratic polygon problems such as $x^4 + 3125 = 125 x^2$ (pp. 207–208).

.. _term-binomiale:

linea binomialis / binomiale — binomial irrational
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (Euclid Book X.36)
* **English Rendering**: *binomial irrational line ($a + \sqrt{b}$ or $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$)*

Compound irrational magnitude formed by adding two lines commensurable in square only, classified into six species in Euclid Book X and tested systematically by Fibonacci in *Distinctio VIII* and *Flos*.

.. _term-recisum:

recisum (apotome) — apotome / residual irrational
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (from Gerard of Cremona's Euclid Book X.73)
* **English Rendering**: *apotome / residual irrational line ($a - \sqrt{b}$ or $\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}$)*

Irrational difference of two lines commensurable in square only—exemplified by the dodecahedron edge $\sqrt{15} - \sqrt{3}$ (a fourth apotome in a sphere of rational diameter $6$, *Distinctio VI*, p. 196) and the decagon chord $\sqrt{31\frac{1}{4}} - 2\frac{1}{2}$ (*Distinctio VIII*, p. 208).

.. _term-congruum:

congruum — common step of three squares in arithmetic progression
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin (Fibonacci, *Flos* & *Liber Quadratorum*, 1225)
* **English Rendering**: *congruum (common difference of three squares in arithmetic progression)*

An integer $D = 4ab(a^2 - b^2)$ (always divisible by $24$) such that $z^2 - D$, $z^2$, and $z^2 + D$ are simultaneously squares. Dividing by $12^2$ yields the rational triple $(\frac{31}{12})^2, (\frac{41}{12})^2, (\frac{49}{12})^2$ with common difference $5$ (*Flos*, p. 227), and connects geometrically to trapezoid bisection $c^2 = (a^2 + b^2)/2$ in *Distinctio IV*.

.. _term-quadrans:

quadrans (oroscopus) — surveyor's quadrant with shadow square
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Tradition**: Medieval Latin
* **English Rendering**: *surveyor's and astronomer's quadrant with shadow square*

Quarter-circle sighting instrument equipped with two pinnules (*tabulae perforatae*), a plumb bob (*perpendiculum*), a 90-degree arc, and an inscribed $12 \times 12$ shadow square (*umbra recta* / *umbra versa*) used in *Distinctio VII* (p. 204) to measure inaccessible tower heights and valley widths by similar right triangles.
