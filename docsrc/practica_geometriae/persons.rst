Index Nominum (Biographical Register)
=====================================

.. rubric:: *Geometers, Algebraists, Translators, Imperial Patrons, and Critical Editors*

This prosopographical register documents **21 historical figures** shaping :person:`Leonardo Pisano <fibonacci>`'s mathematical formation across the Mediterranean, the imperial Sicilian court of :person:`Frederick II <frederick-ii>`, and the Renaissance reception culminating in :person:`Piero della Francesca <piero-della-francesca>` and :person:`Luca Pacioli <pacioli>`.

----

.. _person-fibonacci:

Leonardo Pisano (Fibonacci / Leonardo Bigollo de filiis Bonaccii) (c. 1170 – c. 1245)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Pisan Mathematician & Architect of Western Symbolic Arithmetic and Constructive Geometry*

Educated in Bugia (Béjaïa, North Africa) where his father Guglielmo Bonacci directed the Pisan customs house, Leonardo traveled throughout Egypt, Syria, Greece, Sicily, and Provence studying Indo-Arabic arithmetic, :person:`al-Khwārizmī <al-khwarizmi>`'s and :person:`Abū Kāmil <abu-kamil>`'s algebra, and the Graeco-Arabic geometric canon. Returning to Pisa around 1200, he authored *Liber Abaci* (1202, rev. 1228), *Practica Geometriae* (1220), *Epistola ad Magistrum Theodorum*, *Flos* (1225), and *Liber Quadratorum* (1225), earning an annual civic stipend from the Republic of Pisa in 1241.

.. _person-dominicus:

Master Dominicus (Magister Dominicus) (fl. 1220–1228)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Imperial Court Scholar & Dedicatee of Practica Geometriae*

Revered friend and *magister* to whom Leonardo Pisano dedicated *Practica Geometriae* in 1220 (*"Rogasti, amice Dominice et reverende magister..."*), and who later presented Leonardo to :person:`Emperor Frederick II <frederick-ii>` at Pisa.

.. _person-frederick-ii:

Frederick II of Hohenstaufen (Stupor Mundi) (1194 – 1250)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Holy Roman Emperor & King of Sicily*

Patron of Graeco-Arabic science and philosophy whose court scholars :person:`John of Palermo <john-of-palermo>`, :person:`Michael Scot <michael-scot>`, and :person:`Master Theodorus <theodorus>` posed the Diophantine and cubic challenges to Leonardo Pisano at Pisa around 1225, inspiring *Flos* and *Liber Quadratorum*.

.. _person-john-of-palermo:

John of Palermo (Iohannes Panormitanus) (fl. 1221–1240)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Imperial Court Philosopher & Arabic-Latin Translator*

Philosopher at Frederick II's court who challenged Leonardo Pisano with the cubic equation $x^3 + 2x^2 + 10x = 20$ and the Diophantine :term:`congruum` problem $x^2 \pm 5 = \square$, answered in *Flos* and *Liber Quadratorum* (1225).

.. _person-michael-scot:

Michael Scot (Michael Scottus) (c. 1175 – c. 1232)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Court Astrologer, Philosopher & Dedicatee of the 1228 Liber Abaci*

Translator of Aristotle, Averroes, and al-Biṭrūjī at Toledo and Frederick II's court; Leonardo Pisano dedicated the revised 1228 edition of *Liber Abaci* to him.

.. _person-theodorus:

Master Theodorus of Antioch (d. c. 1250)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Imperial Court Philosopher & Mathematician*

Jacobite Christian scholar trained in Mosul and Baghdad who served as Frederick II's court philosopher and corresponded with Leonardo Pisano on indeterminate equations and inscribed regular polygons.

.. _person-raniero-capocci:

Raniero Capocci of Viterbo (c. 1180 – 1250)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Cardinal Deacon of Santa Maria in Cosmedin & Dedicatee of Flos*

Papal prelate and patron of learning to whom Leonardo Pisano addressed the dedicatory epistle of *Flos* (1225).

.. _person-euclid:

Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Author of the Elements & On Divisions of Figures*

Alexandrian geometer whose *Elements* (Books I–XIII) supply the demonstrative spine of every distinction in *Practica Geometriae*, and whose lost Greek treatise *On Divisions of Figures* (*Peri Diaireseōn*) survives most completely in Latin through Fibonacci's *Distinctio IV*.

.. _person-archimedes:

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287 – 212 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Geometer of Circle Bounds & Sphere-Cylinder Mensuration*

Cited by Fibonacci as *Archimenides phylosophus* (*Distinctio III*, p. 88); his 96-gon polygon bisection in *Measurement of a Circle* and his sphere-and-cylinder theorems underlie *Distinctio III* ($\pi \approx \frac{864}{275}$) and *Distinctio VI*.

.. _person-heron:

Heron of Alexandria (fl. 1st century AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Author of Metrica & Dioptra*

Alexandrian mathematician and engineer whose triangle area formula in terms of the semiperimeter $s$ and excesses $(s-a, s-b, s-c)$ receives a complete Euclidean incenter demonstration in *Practica Geometriae*, *Distinctio III* (pp. 40–41).

.. _person-hypsicles:

Hypsicles of Alexandria (c. 190 – c. 120 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Author of Elements Book XIV*

Alexandrian geometer whose treatise (*Book XIV* of the Euclidean corpus) proved that the dodecahedron and icosahedron inscribed in the same sphere share an identical face circumcircle and satisfy $V_{12} : V_{20} = S_{12} : S_{20} = e_6 : e_{20}$—explicitly cited and demonstrated by Fibonacci at the close of *Distinctio VI* (pp. 197–202).

.. _person-ptolemy:

Claudius Ptolemy (c. 100 – c. 170 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Author of the Almagest*

Alexandrian astronomer and geometer cited repeatedly in *Practica Geometriae* (*Distinctio III, VI, VIII*) for chord tables, cyclic quadrilateral products (Ptolemy's theorem), and spherical geometry.

.. _person-menelaus:

Menelaus of Alexandria ('Mileus') (c. 70 – 140 AD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Geometer of Chords & Sphaerica*

Cited by Fibonacci as *in libro Milei* (*Distinctio VI*, p. 197) alongside the *Almagest* and Euclid Book XIV in the analysis of pentagonal chords and spherical figures.

.. _person-al-khwarizmi:

Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī (c. 780 – c. 850)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Founder of Ḥisāb al-jabr wa-l-muqābala*

Baghdad House of Wisdom mathematician whose six canonical quadratic rules in :term:`res` (*radix*), :term:`census` (*māl*), and *numerus* provide the algebraic engine of *Liber Abaci* Ch. 15 and *Practica Geometriae* *Distinctio VIII*.

.. _person-abu-kamil:

Abū Kāmil Shujāʿ ibn Aslam (c. 850 – c. 930)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Egyptian Algebraist ('The Reckoner')*

Author of *On the Pentagon and Decagon* and the *Book of Algebra*, whose biquadratic equations (*census census*) for inscribing regular polygons inside a circle of diameter $10$ directly inspired *Distinctio VIII*.

.. _person-banu-musa:

Banū Mūsā (Muḥammad, Aḥmad, and al-Ḥasan ibn Mūsā) (9th century)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Baghdad Geometers of Verba filiorum*

Three brothers whose *Book on the Measurement of Plane and Spherical Figures* (translated into Latin by :person:`Gerard of Cremona <gerard-of-cremona>` as *Verba filiorum*) transmitted Heron's incenter proof and Archimedean circle and sphere demonstrations to the Latin West.

.. _person-savasorda:

Abraham bar Ḥiyya ha-Nasi (Savasorda) (c. 1070 – c. 1136)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Author of Ḥibbur ha-Meshīḥah we-ha-Tishboret (Liber embadorum)*

Barcelona mathematician whose Hebrew treatise on practical geometry (translated into Latin as *Liber embadorum* by Plato of Tivoli in 1145) pioneered the transmission of Euclidean field divisions and chord mensuration prior to Fibonacci's vaster 1220 synthesis.

.. _person-gerard-of-cremona:

Gerard of Cremona (c. 1114 – 1187)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Master Translator of the Toledo School*

Translated Euclid's *Elements* (with Books XIV–XV), Ptolemy's *Almagest*, al-Khwārizmī's *Algebra*, Abū Kāmil, Archimedes' *Measurement of a Circle*, and the Banū Mūsā's *Verba filiorum* into Latin, forging key technical terms such as :term:`recisum` (*apotome*).

.. _person-piero-della-francesca:

Piero della Francesca (c. 1415 – 1492)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Renaissance Painter & Geometer*

Adapted Fibonacci's *Practica Geometriae* (especially *Distinctio VI* on the five regular solids in a sphere and *Distinctio VIII* on golden pentagon chords) directly into his *Trattato d'abaco* and *Libellus de quinque corporibus regularibus*.

.. _person-pacioli:

Luca Pacioli, O.F.M. (c. 1447 – 1517)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Renaissance Mathematician & Author of De Divina Proportione*

Explicitly acknowledged Leonardo Pisano as his primary fountainhead across *Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalita* (1494) and *De Divina Proportione* (1509), transmitting Fibonacci's Pisan field geometry, divisions, and golden polyhedral mensuration into Renaissance print.

.. _person-boncompagni:

Baldassarre Boncompagni-Ludovisi (1821 – 1894)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

**Role**: *Historian of Mathematics & Editor of Scritti di Leonardo Pisano*

Roman prince, bibliophile, and historian of science who edited and published the monumental *editio princeps* of Fibonacci's works in two folio volumes (*Romae*, 1857 and 1862) from the Florentine and Milanese manuscripts.
