Positional Extraction of Cube Roots
-----------------------------------

*Latin Locus*: :ref:`Distinctio V (pp. 148–158) <dist-05-la>` · *Commentary*: :ref:`Notes on Cube-Root Extraction <dist-05-s1-notes>`

.. enunciation::

   Here ends the fourth distinction, on the division of fields among co-heirs; here begins the fifth, on finding cube roots. Since according to the promises made in the beginning of this work I must treat of cube roots, of which I inserted a special treatise in the *Book of Calculation* (*Liber Abaci*), I have thought good to rewrite the same here, so that having continued the above distinctions I may more easily arrive at the end of the work.

A cube number is that which arises from the multiplication of three equal numbers, or from any square number multiplied by its root, such as 8 and 27: for 8 arises from the multiplication of 2 by 2 multiplied by 2, or from the multiplication of 4 by its root, namely 2, and 27 arises from
three threes, or from nine multiplied by its root, which is 3. For the cube root of eight is 2, and the cube root of 27 is 3; and so understand concerning the remaining cube numbers and their roots: but the remaining numbers, which are not cubes, cannot have cube roots in numbers;
whence their cube roots are called surds. Now I shall demonstrate how the cube root of any number may be found by approximation.

But first I wish to demonstrate at present whence the method of finding these roots proceeds.
Whenever a line is divided into two parts, the cubes of the parts themselves together with three times the product of the square of each section into the other will be equal to the cube of the whole line. For example: let line .ab. be divided as you please at point .g.
I say that the cubes of the parts .ag. and .gb., together with three times the square of the part .ag. into .gb., and three times the square of the part .bg. into .ga., are equal to the cube of the whole line .ab.; let this be seen in numbers: let the whole .ab. be 5,
and let .ag. be 3; there will remain .gb. as 2; the cubes of which parts are 27 and 8, which joined together make 35; and from three times the square of 3 into 2 come 54, and from three times the square of 2 into 3 come 36; and thus in sum are obtained 125,
namely the cube of 5, that is, of line .ab. For 5 is the cube root of 125, because multiplying 5 by itself makes 25, and multiplying this by 5 makes 125. And having reflected for a long time upon this definition, I discovered this method of finding roots, as I shall explain below.
But first I wish to show how any number ought to be cubed according to this definition, so that if you wish to cube 12, take the cube of 10 and the cube of 2, into which parts 12 is divided, and they will be 1008; upon which add three times the square of 10
multiplied by 2, and three times the square of 2 multiplied by 10, namely 600 and 120, and in sum there will be 1728, which is the cube of 12: and thus you can operate in cubing whatever number you wish. Whence let us return to finding the cube roots of any numbers.
First it must be known which are the cube numbers formed from the numbers of the first rank. For the cube of unity is 1, of two 8, of three 27, of four 64, of five 125, of six 216, of seven 343, of eight 512, of nine 729, and the cube of ten 1000;
when these are known in order by heart, know that the cube root of numbers of one, two, or three figures is one figure; of four, five, or six figures the cube root is a number of two figures; of seven, eight, or nine figures the root is a number of three figures:
and so always thereafter, as a number grows by one, two, or three figures, its root grows by one figure only. These things being understood, it is necessary to teach how to find the difference between any cube number and the one following it; therefore multiply the root of the one by the root of the other,
and triple what results, and add 1 to the sum, which arises from cubing the unit by which the root of the greater cube exceeds the root of the lesser: for example, I wish to know how much the cube made from 3 adds over the cube made from 2: triple the product of 2 into 3,
which will be 18; to which add 1, and it will be 19 for the sought difference: which 19, if added onto the cube made from 2, namely onto 8, will yield 27, namely the cube made from 3. These things explained, to find the root of 47 by approximation,
take the greatest root that 47 has in whole numbers, and it is 3; whose cube, namely 27, subtract from 47, leaving 20; therefore the cube root of 47 is 3, and 20 remains. Let this 3 be line .ab., and you shall proportion 20 to the difference between the cube made from 3 and the cube made from 4;
which difference you will find from three times the product of 3 into 4 with 1 added, or by subtracting 27 from 64; which difference is 37; of which 20 is more than half: wherefore add :math:`\frac{1}{2}` upon line .ab., and let it be .bg.; and let the cube of number .ag. be found;
which will be found thus: I shall cube the parts .ab. and .bg., giving :math:`27\frac{1}{8}`; upon which I shall add three times the square of .ab. into .bg., and three times the square of .gb. into .ba., that is :math:`13\frac{1}{2}` and :math:`2\frac{1}{4}`, giving :math:`42\frac{7}{8}`; which fall short of 47 by :math:`4\frac{1}{8}`;
therefore the cube root of 47 is :math:`3\frac{1}{2}`, and there remains over

In the same way, if you wish to find the root of any number of eight or nine figures, leaving aside the first three figures, strive to find the root of the remaining ones by the demonstrated method; and thereafter, having joined their remainder with the three dismissed figures,
you shall act according to what we have just done, and you will find what is sought. God willing, in the same way and order you will be able to operate in finding the cube roots of numbers of ten or more figures.

When two numbers fall between unity and any number in continued proportion, the first of them is clearly shown in geometry to be the cube root of the last number. Whence if we wish to find geometrically the cube root of any number, we must use the methods of the ancients,
by which it is taught to place two other quantities between two given quantities, so that all four quantities are in one proportion; and although this cannot be worked easily, yet we shall show how it ought to be done. Let there be two given quantities .c. and .f.
We wish to place two quantities between .c. and .f. in continued proportion, and let .f. be greater than .c. I shall set out a circle .abgd., whose diameter .ag. is equal to quantity .f.; and I shall place in circle .abgd. a line .gd. equal to quantity .c.;
and I shall conceive a half-cylinder standing orthogonally upon semicircle .adg., whose half-roundness falls upon the arc .adg.: wherefore the diameter of the base of the cylinder will fall upon diameter .ag., and its endpoints will be upon points .a. and .g.;
and then the plane surface of the half-cylinder will rise orthogonally upon .ag.; and in it I shall draw the semicircle .aeg., whose surface stands orthogonally upon the surface of circle .abgd.; and from point .a. I shall draw straight line .az. making a right angle upon straight line .ga.,
and I shall make it meet line .gd. at point .z.; and I shall move semicircle .aeg. from the part .a.; and I shall revolve triangle .zag. upon straight line .ag., which remains fixed in its revolution; and when I move semicircle .aeg., point .g. will stand fixed;

and as the ratio of .dz. to .de.; of which this is the demonstration. Since the two lines .ze. and .mp. are parallel and equal, and the two angles .zep. and .mpe. are right, then line .zm. is equal to line .ep., and each of the two angles .ezm. and .mpe. is right.
But line .md. is perpendicular upon line .zg., and line .zd. is perpendicular upon line .me. Therefore the ratio of line .gd. to .dm. is as the ratio of .dm. to .dz., and as the ratio of .dz. to .de. Indeed line .gd. is equal to quantity .a., and line .de. is equal to quantity .b.;
therefore the two lines .de. and .dm. have now fallen between the two quantities .a. and .b., and are continued according to one proportion; and that is what we wish to show.

.. rubric:: *Here Begins the Multiplication of Cube Roots Among Themselves*

If you wish to multiply the cube root of 40
by the cube root of 60, multiply 40 by 60, giving 2400, whose cube root is what you seek. And if you wish to multiply 5 by the cube root of 90, cube 5, giving 125: therefore you wish to multiply the cube root of 125 by the cube root of 90;
wherefore you shall multiply 125 by 90, and the cube root of what results is what you seek. And if you wish to multiply two cube roots of 20 by three cube roots of 40, reduce them to the cube root of one number thus: for two roots of 20 you shall cube 2, giving 8;
which multiply by 20, giving 160, whose cube root is equal to two roots of 20: similarly for three roots of 40 cube 3, giving 27; which multiply by 40, giving 1080, whose cube root is taken for three roots of 40: multiply therefore 160 by 1080,
and the cube root of what results will be what you seek. Likewise if you wish to multiply the cube root of 20 by something whence a given number arises, say 10, cube 10, giving 1000; which divide by 20, giving 50,

for example: let 6 be cubed, giving 216; and find two numbers which multiplied together make 216; and they will be 9 and 24, whose cube roots are what is sought. Otherwise let any two square numbers lie adjacent, and let them be 4 and 9;

.. rubric:: *Here Begins the Division of Roots Among Themselves*

If you wish to divide the cube root of 100
by the cube root of 5, divide 100 by 5, yielding 20, whose cube root is what you seek. And if you divide 5 by 100, there will come forth :math:`\frac{1}{20}`, whose cube root is that which results from the root of 5 divided by the root of 100.
And if you wish to divide 8 by the root of 32, divide the cube of 8, namely 512, by 32, yielding 16, whose cube root is what you seek. And if you wish to divide the root of 80 by 2, divide 80 by the cube of 2, yielding 10, whose cube root is what you seek.
Likewise if you wish to divide eight cube roots of 10 by three cube roots of 5, reduce the plurality of the roots themselves to one root, and you will have 5120 for eight roots of 10, and for three roots of 5 will be had the root of 135.

.. rubric:: *Here Begins the Addition and Subtraction of Roots*

Know that in the addition and subtraction of cube roots there happen the same things that happen among square roots, namely that some of them can be added together and subtracted from one another, and some cannot. Therefore when cube roots have to one another a ratio as a cube number to a cube number,
then they can be added together and subtracted. Whence if you wish to add those whose cubes have a ratio like a cube number to a cube number, add the roots of those cubes together, and cube what results, and multiply the cubed sum by the multiplicity that the cubes of those roots have to the cubes of the ratio.
For example: you wish to add the cube root of 16 with the cube root of 54, whose ratio of numbers is as the cube number 8 to the cube number 27; and each of them is double its cube: add therefore the cube root of 8 with the cube root of 27, namely 2 with 3, giving 5;
cube this, giving 125; which multiply by 2 because of 16 and 54, which are doubles of 8 and of 27, giving 250, whose cube root is the sought addition. And if you wish to subtract the roots themselves, subtract the root of 8 from the root of 27, leaving one,
whose cube, namely 1, multiply by the aforesaid multiplier, namely by 2, and you will have the root of 2 for the remainder of the sought subtraction. Likewise if you wish to add the cube root of 4 with the cube root of 32, whose ratio of numbers is as 1 to 8,
and each of them is four times its cube. Wherefore add the cube root of 1 with the root of 8, giving 3; whose cube, namely 27, quadruple, giving 108, whose cube root is the sought addition. And if you wish to subtract the root of 4 from the root of 32,
subtract the root of 1 from the root of 8, leaving 1, whose cube, namely 1, quadruple, giving 4, whose cube root is the remainder of the sought subtraction.
