Part I — Dimension of Prisms, Pyramids, Cones, and Spheres
----------------------------------------------------------

*Latin Locus*: :ref:`Distinctio VI (Pars I) — De Dimensione Corporum (pp. 158–190) <dist-06-solids-la>` · *Commentary*: :ref:`Notes on Archimedes & Stereometry <dist-06-s2-notes>`

.. enunciation::

   Here begins the Sixth Distinction, on the dimension of bodies (*in dimensione corporum*).

There are many kinds of solid bodies, among which are these: rectangular solids, wedges, pyramids, columns (prisms and cylinders), spheres and their parts, and those bodies called many-based which are inscribed or circumscribed about spheres.

1. **Prisms and Cylinders**: Measured by multiplying the area of the base (:term:`embadum`) into the perpendicular altitude (:term:`cathetus`). :just:`[XII. 11] <XII.11>`
2. **Pyramids and Cones**: Every pyramid or cone is exactly **one-third** of the prism or cylinder having the same base and the same altitude: $V = \frac{1}{3} B h$. :just:`[XII. 7, 10] <XII.7>`
3. **Frustums of Pyramids and Cones** (*Pyramis caput abscisa*): Measured by multiplying one-third of the altitude into the sum of the base area $B_1$, the top area $B_2$, and their geometric mean $\sqrt{B_1 B_2}$:

   $$V = \frac{h}{3}\left(B_1 + B_2 + \sqrt{B_1 B_2}\right).$$

4. **The Sphere**: From :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`' treatise *On the Sphere and Cylinder* (:cite:`archimedes:sphere`), the volume of every sphere is **two-thirds** of the circumscribed cylinder whose diameter and altitude equal the diameter $d$ of the sphere:

   $$V = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \left(\frac{11}{14} d^2 \cdot d\right) = \frac{11}{21} d^3.$$

   And the surface area of the sphere is four times the area of its greatest circle: $S = 4 \cdot \frac{11}{14} d^2 = \frac{22}{7} d^2$.

Part II — The Five Regular Polyhedra Inscribed in a Sphere of Diameter $6$
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Latin Locus*: :ref:`De Quinque Corporibus in Spera Descriptis (pp. 190–196) <dist-06-polyhedra-la>` · *Commentary*: :ref:`Notes on Euclid Book XIII & Piero della Francesca <dist-06-s3-notes>`

.. enunciation::

   To find the edges, areas, and corporeal volumes of the five regular solid bodies of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s Book XIII (:cite:`euclid:elements`) inscribed in a sphere whose diameter is $6$ *perticae*. :just:`[XIII. 13–18] <XIII.18>`

.. picture:: /_static/figures/euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra.svg
   :alt: The Five Regular Polyhedra Inscribed in Sphere Diameter 6
   :caption: Semicircle ( O A ) on sphere diameter AI = 6 (center O, AK = 2): tetrahedron edge / B I / (Q = 24), octahedron / A C / (Q = 18), cube / A B / (Q = 12), dodecahedron / A N / (Q = 18 - sqrt(180)), and icosahedron / M I / (Q = 18 - sqrt(64 4/5)).

Let diameter $AI = 6$ with center :g:`O <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:O>` in semicircle :g:`(OA) <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:( O A )>`. Set $AK = 2$ (one-third of $AI$), and erect perpendiculars :g:`KB <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:/ K B />` and :g:`OC <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:/ O C />`. :just:`[XIII. 18] <XIII.18>`

1. **Tetrahedron** ($4$ Triangular Faces): Edge :g:`BI <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:/ B I />`, whose square is $AI \cdot KI = 6 \times 4 = 24$ ($e_4 = \sqrt{24} = 2\sqrt{6}$), altitude $4$, and volume $V = \sqrt{192} = 8\sqrt{3}$. :just:`[XIII. 13] <XIII.13>`
2. **Cube** ($6$ Square Faces): Edge :g:`AB <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:/ A B />`, whose square is $AI \cdot AK = 6 \times 2 = 12$ ($e_6 = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$), and volume $V = \sqrt{1{,}728} = 24\sqrt{3}$. :just:`[XIII. 15] <XIII.15>`
3. **Octahedron** ($8$ Triangular Faces): Edge :g:`AC <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:/ A C />`, whose square is $AO^2 + OC^2 = 3^2 + 3^2 = 18$ ($e_8 = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$), composed of two square pyramids, yielding the **exact integer rational volume** $V = 36$! :just:`[XIII. 14] <XIII.14>`

Part III — Extreme and Mean Ratio: Dodecahedron and Icosahedron
---------------------------------------------------------------

*Latin Locus*: :ref:`Modus Dividendi Lineam Media et Extrema Proportione (pp. 196–202) <dist-06-dodeca-la>` · *Commentary*: :ref:`Notes on Hypsicles' XIV & Pacioli <dist-06-s3-notes>`

.. picture:: /_static/figures/dodecahedron_golden_chord.svg
   :alt: Dividing the Cube Edge in Extreme and Mean Ratio for the Dodecahedron Edge
   :caption: Division of pentagon chord BE = sqrt(12) in extreme and mean ratio by perpendicular / E C / = sqrt(3), circle ( C E ) and arc ( B D ), giving dodecahedron edge / B G / = / A B / = / A E / = sqrt(15) - sqrt(3).

Because the chord of the regular pentagon in the dodecahedron face is the cube edge $BE = \sqrt{12}$, Leonardo solves the edge of the regular dodecahedron by dividing $BE$ in **extreme and mean ratio** (:term:`media et extrema proportione <media-et-extrema-proportio>`) :just:`[VI. 30, XIII. 17] <XIII.17>`:

* Bisect $BE$ at :g:`M <dodecahedron_golden_chord:M>`, and through circle :g:`(EM) <dodecahedron_golden_chord:( E M )>` erect perpendicular :g:`EC <dodecahedron_golden_chord:/ E C />` $= \frac{1}{2} BE = \sqrt{3}$.
* Hypotenuse :g:`BC <dodecahedron_golden_chord:[ B D ]>` has square $12 + 3 = 15$.
* Deducting radius $CD = \sqrt{3}$ by circle :g:`(CE) <dodecahedron_golden_chord:( C E )>` leaves :g:`BD <dodecahedron_golden_chord:[ B D ]>` $= \sqrt{15} - \sqrt{3}$.
* Transferring $BD$ by circle :g:`(BD) <dodecahedron_golden_chord:( B D )>` to :g:`BG <dodecahedron_golden_chord:/ B G />` yields the **exact edge of the dodecahedron**, an :term:`apotome <recisum>` (Euclid X Book irrational):

  $$e_{12} = BG = \sqrt{15} - \sqrt{3}, \qquad e_{12}^2 = 18 - \sqrt{180} = 18 - 6\sqrt{5}.$$

  Its volume is $V_{12} = 30\sqrt{3} + 6\sqrt{15}$.

* For the **Icosahedron** ($20$ Triangular Faces): Through radius :g:`OM <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:/ O M />`, chord :g:`MI <euclid_xiii_sphere_polyhedra:/ M I />` gives:

  $$e_{20}^2 = 18 - \sqrt{64\frac{4}{5}} = 18 - \frac{18}{5}\sqrt{5}, \qquad V_{20} = 18\sqrt{10 + 2\sqrt{5}}.$$

* **Hypsicles' Book XIV Ratio**: Following :person:`Hypsicles <hypsicles>`, the ratio of the volumes (and surface areas) of the dodecahedron and icosahedron inscribed in the same sphere is:

  $$\frac{V_{12}}{V_{20}} = \frac{S_{12}}{S_{20}} = \frac{\text{Cube Edge}}{\text{Icosahedron Edge}} = \sqrt{\frac{5 + \sqrt{5}}{6}} \approx 1.098\dots$$
